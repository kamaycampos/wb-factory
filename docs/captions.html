“What's leaching into your food”
KT_BLACKPLASTICR2/01_WHATS-LEACHING-INTO-YOUR-FOOD_68s.mp4
Kevin Trudeau points to research on black plastic: the kind used in takeout containers, kitchen utensils, and the trays under your grocery store meat and sushi.

That black color often comes from recycled electronics plastic, carrying flame retardants never meant to touch food. Testing has found tiny plastic particles turning up in brain and fat tissue.

Kevin's advice: skip the black plastic. Ask for paper, choose glass or stainless for leftovers, and move food off black trays before you store or reheat it.

Kevin Trudeau says real vitamin E isn't one single ingredient - it's a family.

His example: years ago, scientists made a lab version of vitamin E called D-alpha-tocopherol and sold it as the real thing. Later, researchers who studied whole foods like walnuts found that natural vitamin E is actually made of four different tocopherols, working together.

Next time you pick up a vitamin E bottle, look for 'mixed tocopherols' on the label, not just one name.
